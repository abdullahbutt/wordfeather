telc B1 Sprechen: Ablauf, Uhr und Vorlagen

Speaking cannot be scored by a page, so this is a reference page: the facts, a preparation clock, a speaking clock with a lap for each Teil, the 54 lines to learn by heart, a look-up tab and the Teil 3 worked examples.

🌐 SpracheLanguage
The exam (Mündlicher Ausdruck)
  • You take it with a partner. It lasts about 15 minutes. Before it you have 20 minutes to prepare.
  • Teil 1: you get to know each other (name, country, family, work, hobbies, why you learn German). Ask questions too.
  • Teil 2: each of you gets a card with one person's opinion. You present your card, say your opinion and ask your partner.
  • Teil 3: you both get the same card (for example a party or a trip) and plan together.

    The six typical planning points. Almost every card asks the same questions in different clothes:
    Wann? Day, time, how long. Say a real day and a real hour, not "irgendwann".
    Wo? The place, and why that place: at my flat, in the park, in a restaurant.
    Was mitbringen / was kaufen? Food, drinks, equipment, tickets, tools.
    Wen einladen / wen fragen? How many people, which people, who tells them.
    Wie viel Geld? A number, and how you split it: "Wir teilen die Kosten. Jeder zahlt 20 Euro."
    Wer organisiert was? Divide the jobs: "Ich kaufe ein, du reservierst."
    Die Reihenfolge, die Punkte bringt: Nimm die Punkte der Karte einen nach dem anderen. Bei jedem Punkt: 1. Du machst einen Vorschlag (Wir könnten … / Ich schlage vor, dass wir …). 2. Dein Partner reagiert, du hörst zu und fragst nach (Was meinst du?). 3. Ihr einigt euch (Gute Idee! / Da hast du recht.). Dann kommt der nächste Punkt. Am Ende fasst du alles zusammen: Also machen wir es so: …The order that scores: take the card’s points one by one. For each point: 1. you make a suggestion (We could … / I suggest that we …). 2. Your partner reacts; you listen and ask back (What do you think?). 3. You agree (Good idea! / You are right there.). Then the next point. At the end you sum it all up: So let us do it like this: …
    Tips and traps
    • React first, then add. Do not answer a suggestion with a brand new suggestion. Say "Gute Idee" or "Das sehe ich anders, weil ..." first, then propose something.
    • Ask a question after almost every idea ("Was meinst du?", "Wärst du damit einverstanden?"). The examiners listen for a dialogue.
    • No monologue: two or three sentences, then hand over.
    • Use Konjunktiv II for polite suggestions: wir könnten, wie wäre es, ich hätte Lust auf. It sounds more B1 than "wir machen das".
    • Disagree at least once, politely: "Im Prinzip ja, aber ...", then offer an alternative.
    • Cover every bullet on the card and reach a decision for each one. Say it out loud: "Gut, dann Samstag um vier." About one minute per bullet is a safe rhythm.
    • Finish before the time is over: "Fassen wir zusammen ..." An unfinished plan is the most common way to lose points.
    • Give concrete details: "Am Samstag um vier bei mir" beats "irgendwann bei jemandem". Names, times, prices and places all count.
    • No word for something? Describe it ("so ein Ding, mit dem man Fleisch grillt"). Stopping dead is worse than a simple word.
    • Keep your partner in the conversation: "Und du, hast du noch eine Idee?"
    • Do not learn a fixed speech. Learn the six-step skeleton and fill it with whatever card you get.
